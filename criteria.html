Core Ecosystem Criteria for 2055

How Los Baños balances public health, passive bioclimatic building, lakeshore wetland restoration, and participatory governance as one living human ecological system.

Clean Air & Healthy Neighborhoods

HEALTH

Removing smoke-belching combustion traffic and growing continuous tree cover has dramatically reduced asthma, heatstroke, and heart illnesses across our barangays.

  • Breathe Clean Air: Electric jeepneys and solar shuttles keep local air within clean, healthy WHO guidelines.
  • Cool Shade Everywhere: Continuous street trees and narra canopies lower neighborhood temperatures naturally by up to 3°C.
  • Walkable 15-Minute Town: Shaded walking paths and bike lanes allow residents to reach markets, health centers, and schools safely without cars.

Naturally Cool Homes & Clean Energy

HOUSING

Housing is built to withstand strong typhoons and stay breezy in the summer using local materials, natural ventilation, and clean rooftop energy.

  • Built for Mountain Breezes: Wide eaves, high ceilings, and cross-ventilation keep homes cool without expensive air conditioning.
  • Sturdy Local Bamboo: Sustainable engineered bamboo and low-carbon materials reduce reliance on heavy, heat-absorbing concrete.
  • Solar & Geothermal Power: Rooftop solar panels paired with clean geothermal baseload power keep lights on even during typhoon blackouts.

Clean Rivers & Living Lake Shorelines

WATERSHED

Nature serves as our flood and water defense. Instead of ugly concrete walls, living plants protect our mountain slopes and filter lake water.

  • Living Creek Banks: Native bamboo and deep-rooted vetiver grass hold riverbanks firm along Molawin Creek during flash rains.
  • Natural Wetland Filters: Shoreline reed beds and gravel ponds filter domestic greywater before it enters Laguna de Bay.
  • Mount Makiling Agroforestry: Diverse fruit trees, cacao, and coffee protect the upper watershed soil from landslides.

Community Bayanihan & Fair Water Sharing

COMMUNITY

Vital resources are managed democratically by residents through Bayanihan cooperatives, making sure no family is left behind during dry seasons.

  • Shared Rainwater Tanks: Neighborhood water cooperatives collect rain in large cisterns and manage fair household allowances.
  • Community Food Plots: Shared rooftop and purok vegetable gardens provide fresh, affordable food for all families.
  • Bayanihan Disaster Brigades: Neighborhood volunteers manage prepositioned relief supplies and mutual aid shelters for typhoons.
