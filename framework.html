Academic Core Concept Human Ecology (HUME 100)

The Human Ecology Framework

Technology alone cannot save the place we are living in. It must be interconnected with the people and structures we create. In order to see actual tangible developments in communities, the social system and ecosystem must work together to bring development.

Theoretical Model

The Human Ecological Framework

Based on the foundational Human Ecology model: the Social System and the Ecosystem function as two complex systems linked by flows of material, energy, information, human activities, and services.

Figure 1. The Human Ecosystem Interaction Model

01. The Social System

The social system includes all the people living in the place. It also includes the structures made by them, the knowledge they bring, and their overall impact to the community.

In an ideal future, Los Baños is not managed by corporate systems. Instead, we have figured out a way to incorporate the actual community in the development of the place. Through assemblies, action, and discussions, we have enabled proper sharing of resources, education of cleaner energy, and the importance of using sustainable resources.

02. The Ecosystem

Their ecosystem represents the part of the community other than the social system. It represents the living and physical environment, namely plants, animals, water, air, and the overall environment we live in.

These parts are naturally interconnected. It is the social system’s job in how to utilize these resources without severe disruption of natural processes.

System Interactions

How this framework works to solve the problems of Los Baños

This framework solves problems by understanding that the community is interconnected. Instead of separating the community into three individual parts, we analyze and utilize resources in the view that everything in the community is united as one.

The social systen are the actors that are responsible for development. They are the ones to utilize what is in the ecosystem to be utilized by the people. While all of this is true, it also the social system’s job to limit the resources and not disrupt any natural processes.

In this world where we have perfectly incorporated the human ecological framework, we analyze anything we develop in conjunction with three different aspects. How will it affect humanities’ lives? Will it help or disrupt any natural processes? Are the development important enough for the geographical space to be used for it? These are three considerations when building structures for development, although there are significantly more factors in actually making sure they are effective and sustainable.
