The Human Ecology Framework

The core philosophy introduced in our letter from 2055: Why technology and high-tech gadgets alone could not stop climate disasters, and why Los Baños survived by turning away from private profit and embracing Human Ecology—treating our community well-being, engineered housing, and Mount Makiling's ecosystems as one living whole.

Theoretical Model

The Human Ecological Framework

Based on the foundational Human Ecology model (Marten, 2001): Human society (the Social System) and the biophysical environment (the Ecosystem) function as two complex adaptive systems linked by reciprocal flows of material, energy, information, human activities, and ecosystem services.

Figure 1. The Human Ecosystem Interaction Model

01. The Social System

The Social System encompasses everything created and practiced by the people of Los Baños—including our community knowledge, technology, population, social organization, and cultural values.

Instead of relying on profit-driven corporate systems, our society is organized around Bayanihan cooperation. Through weekly barangay assemblies and watershed education, residents share resources like water and clean power responsibly, design cool homes using local bamboo, and make decisions that protect future generations.

02. The Ecosystem

The Ecosystem represents the living and physical environment of the Mount Makiling watershed—including plants, animals, micro-organisms, air, water, soil, and the physical buildings we live in.

Every part of this landscape is interconnected. Primary rainforests on Makiling stabilize mountain soils and replenish groundwater aquifers, mountain breezes circulate fresh air into the lowlands, and restored shoreline wetlands naturally filter water flowing into Laguna de Bay.

System Interactions

How this framework works to solve the problems of Los Baños

In Los Baños, human society and nature solve climate crises by treating every community action and environmental response as one connected loop. Instead of allowing urban sprawl to destroy the slopes of Mount Makiling, citizens choose compact living, active watershed reforestation, and community-led monitoring that directly protects biodiversity.

In return, healthy natural ecosystems provide essential daily services: clean drinking water from mountain aquifers, fresh fish from Laguna de Bay, natural cooling breezes, and forest canopies that prevent catastrophic flooding during typhoons.

Information and resources circulate continuously between both spheres. Composted bio-nutrients nourish agricultural soils, while environmental data—such as seasonal rainfall and aquifer levels—guide fair, cooperative resource sharing so that no household exceeds what the watershed can naturally regenerate.
